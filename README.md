# CyberDefense: AI

A base-defense game about light and darkness, built mobile-first. One page and its sound cues in `sfx/`, no build step. Open `index.html` in a browser to play.

**Play:** https://zoeallgaier.github.io/viscera/

On iPhone, open it in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen. It launches full-screen like an app.

## The loop
- **Light is territory.** Servers chain power out from the Base. You can only build within cable reach of a powered server, and turrets only fire at bots standing in light.
- **Data cuts both ways.** Every powered server, turret, launcher, Auger and Warden (and the Base) adds 1 Data. Each point adds 4% to turret, missile and EMP damage, and each wave grows with your Data.
- **Lithium is your budget.** Everything costs whole points: Server 1, Refinery 2, Turret 3, Missiles 4, Auger 5, Warden 12. You start with 10. Each refinery on a glowing pool earns 1 Lithium every 8 seconds, and every wave you clear (last bot down) pays 2, plus 1 more for every 3 waves held, up to 6 per wave from wave 12 on. Pools run dry (the two by the Base go first, around waves 3–5), so you have to push light out into the void to reach richer ones. A green gauge under each refinery shows what's left in its pool, and a dry one goes dark and reads DRY; scrap it for its full 2 Li back.
- **Turrets vs. missiles.** Turrets fire fast at close range (4.2 tiles). Missile launchers reach 5.2 tiles and fire a homing missile every 2.2 seconds at the biggest cluster in light, with splash damage. Infiltrators take half damage from missiles, full from turrets.
- **A piece's card.** Tap any piece on the board and its card comes up: its field guide entry (sprite, name, what it does, its readouts), cloned from the guide itself so the two never disagree, with its HP live in the name row. The piece is ringed on the board with its range. The card docks to the bottom of the map, or the top when the piece stands down there. Tap off it, or the piece again, to close it. A card for a piece with no upgrade on offer ends with what scrapping it pays back (and a refinery's, what's left in its pool).
- **Upgrades** (code: refits). They open once wave 11 is held (*Upgrades online*), three waves before the first Megapanzer (wave 15). Each weapon upgrades once, in place: its card shows the upgrade's entry under its own, each readout straight over the one it replaces, and asks *Not now* or *Upgrade · N Li*. Before wave 12 the card still shows the upgrade, but the button reads *From wave 12*. Dropping a piece on its twin upgrades it too. An upgrade keeps the piece's one point of Data, so it adds firepower without growing the waves. Scrapping one refunds both prices. The upgraded piece prints in again from the ground as its new self.
  - **Railgun** (Turret +8 Li, 100 HP): two long rails wound with three purple coils, on a base with a lit purple rim. Every 1.5 s it fires one slug at the nearest bot in light (5.6 tiles) that runs out to full range and hits every bot in its line for 60, lit or not. The slug is a hard white line that steps down the purple ramp with a spiral of charge around it.
  - **Ion Launcher** (Missiles +10 Li, 110 HP): a swarm launcher. A wider, squatter pod whose face is a honeycomb of twelve cells, each holding a lit ion charge, with a lit coil band; conduit posts stand at the base's corners. It reaches 7 tiles and every 3 s lets go a swarm of 5 darts, 0.09 s apart, spread over the biggest crowds that don't overlap. Each dart fans out to one side, loops the loop on the spot (up and over as it turns), then dives, turning harder the longer it chases, and bursts wide: 22 damage in a 2.1-tile blast. A dart is a white-hot bead trailing the curl of its own path.
  - **Overload** (the EMP, +14 Li, once, from the Base's card). Every EMP after it does 40 instead of 15, stuns for 7 s instead of 5, and recharges in 12 s instead of 18, and a second wavefront follows the first. A pulse still costs 6. The pad piece takes a ringed glyph and reads *Overload*.
  - The first calm you could afford an upgrade you haven't seen, a toast points it out, once per run.
- **The Auger** (5 Li, 60 HP) and the two **deep wells** it drills arrive together on wave 22. Until then there are no wells on the map, and the Auger's pad piece reads *Wave 22* where the price goes. When wave 21 is held, a toast says *Deep wells surfacing* and the ground gives, one well a beat after the other. Each well starts with a rumble of dust. Then it breaks with a deep thud and a harder shake, throwing a ring of dust and clods of earth, and cracks open in stepped stages, its cracks racing out with white-hot tips. Last it spouts: a column of Lithium punches up out of the bore, drops rain back, three green rings run out, and green motes drift off it. Then the Auger's pad piece prints in from the bottom, *Auger online · 2 deep wells*, and the bores blink green for a few seconds so you can find them in the void.
  - A deep well is a dark, sunken patch of slab, cracked out from a bore hole, with dim green veins that glint and still show in the void. Wells come up 6.5–13 tiles out, clear of the pools and of your pieces (if the board is too built up for that, the patch may run under them, but never the bore).
  - An Auger dropped anywhere on a well's patch goes on its bore, and while you hold one every free well is ringed.
  - It's a screw lift at 2 × 2, standing on three legs. A screw with clean Lithium flights turns up out of a steel collar on the bore, a bright band climbing it into the gearbox head, which carries one row of Data LEDs, the motor behind and the gold power light on top.
  - It earns 1 Li every 10 seconds forever: a pool runs dry, a deep well never does. It's wired in and loud, so it makes 1 Data, and Infiltrators hunt it like a refinery.
- **The Warden** (code: `mech`; 12 Li, 150 HP) opens on wave 28. Until then it isn't on the pad. When wave 27 is held, the Base calls it up: the radome flashes, three purple rings run out from it, the pad piece prints in on its plinth, and the comms say *Warden online · it patrols its dock*. Key 6 picks it.
  - It's a turret and a launcher in one, on legs. You build its **dock** (a low hardstand with a purple ring round its cradle and a lit post at each side), and the Warden patrols a ring 1.75 tiles round it. Its weapons reach from wherever it stands: a **burst cannon** (3 rounds of 10, 0.075 s apart, every 0.8 s, at the nearest bot in light within 3.8 tiles) and a **missile pod** (a pair of 16-damage missiles, 1.5-tile blast, every 3.4 s at the biggest crowd within 4.6). Infiltrators take half from the missiles, as from any. It carries its own light (2 tiles, round the walker, not the dock), and it makes 1 Data.
  - **It isn't a sitting target.** Bots chase its body, not its dock, and a Panzer's blast or a Megapanzer's crush catches it only where it stands. Three times a second it scores the spots in its ring (and where it stands, and its dock) for risk: bots within 1.5 tiles, any Panzer within its 2.2-tile blast (weighted heaviest), a Megapanzer lane ahead of the boss (or one that's just been called), and ground the Borer has marked. It picks the safest spot that keeps a 2.6-tile standoff from what it's shooting and isn't a long walk. When the spot it's on is risky it **dashes** on its jets (1.6 tiles/s against a 0.85 walk) for up to 1.1 s, recharging when it isn't. It walks round other pieces, never through them or across holes.
  - With nothing about it walks a **round**: point to point round its ring, one way, pausing at each and looking about. Out of power (it's fed at its dock), it walks home and **kneels**, dark; powered again, it stands before it walks.
  - **The look.** A graphite biped about twice a turret's height: two-bone legs with the knees bending forward, a purple light in each knee cap, a purple band across the pelvis; a hunched chest with a purple band round it; a head sunk between broad pauldrons with one purple visor slit; the burst cannon on the near arm, its shroud a step darker so it reads even pointing at you; a 2 × 2 missile pod on the far shoulder; a reactor pack with vents, two jets and a whip antenna on its back. The legs are rendered at 16 headings in 8 poses (a six-frame stride, standing, kneeling); the upper body turns on its own at 32 headings, so it aims while it walks.
  - **Micro-motion.** The stride is paced to the ground so the feet never skate, the upper body bobs a pixel with it, and each footfall kicks dust. Turning on the spot, it steps round. Locking onto a new bot, its visor flares white with a purple cross. Each round of a burst kicks the upper body back a pixel, flashes the muzzle and throws a brass casing off the arm; it slows to brace while it fires. The pod's four warheads go dark when the pair is away and light again halfway through the reload. Dashing, its jets burn purple under its back. A shadow follows it, and the hold, card and scrap marks sit on its dock.
  - Tap the walker for its card: HP, and its damage (the burst, then the missiles, with Data's boost). While its card is open or you're holding it, it holds still. Scrapping it refunds 12; the walker and its dock de-print apart, the outline on the dock.
- **Waves get worse.** Bots gain health every wave and runners become more common. Bot types: Walker, Runner, Panzer (wave 3+; bursts and snuffs nearby servers for 10 seconds, which cuts power to everything downstream), Infiltrator (wave 6+; hunts refineries and shrugs off half of missile damage), Sniper (wave 15+, see below).
- **The Sniper.** The Infiltrator's spotter, from wave 15. Wave 15 is the Megapanzer alone, so the first one comes on wave 16, and that wave always leads with one; after that each bot has about a 6% chance of being one (rising to 10%), and an Infiltrator raid brings Snipers in place of its Runners. It's the Drone and the Infiltrator's child: the Drone's two legs, box body and box head, grown only to a crouch and cast in the Infiltrator's near-black, wearing the Infiltrator's swept wedge as a mantle over its shoulders, with the Infiltrator's red slit for a visor and its thruster as a red vent on its back. A long rifle with a muzzle brake runs out of its chest. It hunts **servers** (anything you've built once there are none), but never walks in: it picks a perch out in the void at least half a tile clear of your light and within 4.6 tiles of the target, slinks there, settles for 1.4 s, then paints the target with a dotted red laser that closes up and brightens over 0.9 s before the round lands (13 damage, one every 3.2 s; a server falls in four). Settled in the void, it draws a cloak: its body steps down to a ghost while its lens stays lit. Your guns only fire into your light, so it's out of reach until you **push light out over its perch** (or a Railgun's slug runs on into the void through it). Once lit, it gives up the perch and slinks off to a fresh one. With nowhere dark left in range of anything, it walks into range and shoots from the light. It walks, so it steps round the Borer's holes like a Drone. 45 HP at wave 1, growing 12% a wave like the rest.
- **Boss waves.** A boss wave is the boss alone: no other bots spawn (the Borer's Runners are its own), so the boss carries the wave's weight in its own health. The cadence: one Megapanzer on 15 and 25, one Borer on 30 (its debut), then a boss every 5th wave, alternating (Megapanzers on 35, 45, 55…, Borers on 40, 50, 60…). Wave 20 is an ordinary wave. Themed waves fall on 5, 7, 12, then one per 5-wave stretch. From wave 35 every boss wave sends **two**: two Megapanzers on 35, 45, 55…, two Borers on 40, 50, 60…. Each of a pair has 75% of a lone boss's health, so a pair is half again the health, split across two fronts. Two Megapanzers take parallel lanes on the same axis at least 6 rows apart, rolling opposite ways, the second 4 seconds behind the first. Two Borers tunnel in 6 seconds apart, about half a bite cycle, so one is usually up while the other is below, and they never mark overlapping chunks.
- **The Megapanzer.** A boss on wave 15 and every 10th wave after (25, 35, 45…), so it never meets the Borer's waves (30, 40, 50…). It's a siege crawler 2 tiles across and 3 long, with a tread under each row, a crusher drum and a caged red core. Its health grows 12% a wave from wave 1, like the other bots, so it arrives with about 2,410 HP on wave 15 (2,950 on wave 20, 3,490 on wave 25; each of wave 35's pair has about 3,430). It doesn't hunt: it takes a random two-row lane (not based on what you've built), the lane flashes red for 3 seconds, then it cuts across the board on a diagonal (along the tile lines) end to end, never turning, and crushes everything in its two rows for 15 damage a second. A pass over the Base does 250. Kill it and it bursts (30 damage to buildings within 2.6 tiles, servers snuffed) and pays a 3 Li bounty. The EMP stuns it for half as long. The wave isn't held until it's dead or has rolled off the far edge.
- **The Borer.** The boss on wave 30 and every 10th wave after (40, 50…), in place of the Megapanzer. It's a mechanical earthworm in brushed silver, the drones' cold slate a step brighter: ring segments with dark joints, a slate saddle band whose joints burn red, four rows of bristle hooks with bright tips, a red optic down each flank on every other segment. Its head is a flared bell that tips up toward the camera: four hooked slate claws, a crown of six optics, and three rings of silver fangs around a red gullet. It's alive all the time it's up, in a one-second loop: the neck weaves a slow circle, the claws splay open and curl shut while the gullet flares, a light runs round the crown, the inner rings of fangs grind against each other, a swallow runs down the body lighting its joints red, and the bristles ripple. It shudders as it grinds out of the ground and again just before it dives, sheds earth while it's up, and spits sparks from its joints once it's below half health. It has 1,500 HP at wave 1 and grows 12% a wave, so about 6,720 on wave 30 (each of wave 40's pair has about 6,390). It bites three times:
  - **Tunnelling.** It comes in from the board's edge under a trail of heaving ground (dust rings, flying clods and a furrow of turned-up earth). Then it lurks under a chunk of your ground, about 9–14 tiles somewhere in your light and clear of the Base. The chunk flashes red for 4 seconds, faster in the last 1.5 while the field shakes. Anything you scrap there in time comes back at full refund.
  - **The bite.** It erupts and swallows the chunk: every building on it and any pool goes down its throat, and the tiles become a hole for the rest of the run. You can't build on a hole. Walking bots step round the rims, and Infiltrators hover over them. Each hole also cracks open a fresh 30 Li pool on a free tile beside it.
  - **Up.** It rears out of the hole facing the Base for 7 seconds and spits a brood of Runners (2, then 3, then 4). It can only be hit while it's up. Light anywhere near its body counts, so it can't hide by eating the server that lit it. Then it shudders for a moment, earth pouring off it, and dives: the rim gives way round it as it goes. Then it tunnels to the next chunk.
  - **The EMP** stuns it wherever it is and freezes its clock. Underground that holds off the bite; above ground it pins the Borer up for the guns. Underground the EMP does no damage.
  - **The end.** Kill it for an 8 Li bounty. If it survives all three bites it goes deep, and the next Borer comes back carrying the same share of damage (at least half health); if both of a pair get away, the next two each carry their own. The wave isn't held until it's dead or gone.
  - **Boss plate.** While a boss is on the board, a plate under the console shows its name and a long red bar, plus what it's doing. A pair shares the plate: the name reads *×2* while both are still in it, the bar is what's left of the pair's health, and the state follows the one to act on (a Borer that's up before one that's below). The states: for the Borer, *Bite 2 of 3 coming*, *Exposed*, *Pinned* or *Diving*; for the Megapanzer, *Crushing* or *Stunned*.

## Controls
**Building:** drag a piece from the pad onto the board, or tap a tool and then tap a tile. While you carry a piece:
- Every tile it can go on is tinted purple, with the edge of that zone dotted. Tapping a tool shows its zone for a moment too.
- The piece stands on the tile it will land on, with its range ring and the cable it would draw power through.
- A tip above it gives the piece and its price (and what's left in the pool, for a refinery), or says why it can't go there.
- On touch the piece rides about three and a half tile heights (64px at least) above your fingertip, so the tile stays in view. Each new tile gives a light haptic tick.
- Let go off the board, or press Esc, to cancel.

Tapping a piece you can't afford, or the EMP while it recharges, says what it needs.

**Scrapping:** press and hold a building. Its outline fills in and a tip fills a bar. At about half a second the scrap arms: the tip shows the refund and how many buildings would lose power, and those are marked with a bone dash. Let go to scrap it. To keep it, slide a finger's width away before you let go; sliding back arms it again. The Base can't be scrapped.

**Score:** waves held. Your best is saved on the device.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6 | Server / Refinery / Turret / Missiles / Auger / Warden (keys follow pad order; the pad labels are prices) |
| Tap a piece | Its card; for a weapon or the Base from wave 12, *Upgrade* (Railgun, Ion Launcher, Overload). Esc closes it |
| Right-click, X or Delete (or long-press) | Dismantle the building under the pointer (full refund) |
| Esc | Drop the piece you're carrying, or let go of a hold |
| Space | Pause |
| M (or the speaker in the top bar) | Sound on / off (remembered on the device) |

## Look
The full rules, and the research behind them, are in `research_notes/Isometric pixel art design system/`.
- **Board:** one isometric grid, and nothing else. Tiles are 32 × 16 pixel diamonds in 2:1 dimetric projection, the pixel-art standard, so every edge steps cleanly two across and one down. The board is the rectangle of whole tiles that fills the screen. Every position, range, speed and radius is measured in tiles. The Base covers the centre 3 × 3 tiles; every other piece covers one.
- **One renderer:** every board sprite is a small voxel model, 16 units per tile across and 1 pixel per unit of height. It is drawn through the same projection as the ground, so pieces stand exactly on their tiles. Turret and launcher heads are rendered at 32 headings, and bots at 8 headings with 2 frames each. Each face keeps one flat shade at any heading.
- **Light and shading:** light comes from the upper left. Tops are lightest, faces toward the lower left are mid, and faces toward the lower right are darkest. Each material is a 5-step ramp whose hue shifts warmer toward the light. Top edges that meet a wall catch a lit ledge, and walls darken where they meet the ground. Every standing piece has a 1px dark outline; ground decals like pools have none. There is no dither.
- **Colour means something:** purple is yours, red is theirs.
  - **Yours:** purple light and power (servers, cables, lit windows, turret bands, missiles, the EMP), on warm graphite and weathered concrete. Everything you build carries a purple light.
  - **Theirs:** every bot class shares one family, cold slate or oxblood bodies with red optics and cores. The classes are told apart by shape. Red only ever means bots and the damage they do. A tile you can't build on is a dim dashed outline, and refusals are never red.
  - **Lithium** and **Data** share one phosphor green. The Server's LEDs are electric blue (`#3cc2ee`), so a rack never reads as Lithium.
  - **The world** is one dark, low-chroma grade: near-black warm greys, ochre highlights, muted olive moss. It is muted so the meaning colours stand out, and the UI uses the same grade (warm ink and parchment, the same four accents).
  - Palette: purple `#9b6ef0`, Lithium and Data `#7fd48a`, hostile red `#ff5d57`, ink `#0c0a0a`, parchment `#eee4d4`.
- **The Base:** a 1990s listening station gone to seed. A geodesic radome on a concrete drum is the landmark. Beside it are a low brutalist ops block with a radar bar turning on its roof, a turf-roofed bunker with a lit blast door, two ground dishes, and a short lattice tower carrying the gold beacon. A helipad, a guard hut and boom, and a chain-link fence complete it, with moss on the roofs, vines down the walls and trees moving back in.
- **Drawing order:** everything is sorted by ground depth. Lit pixels (windows, LEDs, optics, beacons) go on their own layer, so they glow in the dark without showing through anything in front of them.
- **Damage you can see:** damage is cut into the voxel models themselves and rendered through the same light. Below two-thirds health a piece has a bite out of it and soot on its walls. Below one-third it has bigger holes, dead lights and a few embers, and it smokes and sparks. A chunk blows off each time it drops a stage. A destroyed building leaves rubble, and when the Base falls its dark shell goes up in a chain of blasts.
- **Effects:** two layers. Debris, smoke and spent casings belong to the world, so the void shades them. Fire, sparks, tracers and hit flashes are light, drawn at full strength on top. Your fire is purple and theirs is red. Every fade steps down a colour ramp, and screen shake moves the view by whole pixels.
- **The EMP:** purple sparks gather in the radome, a beam fires from it, and a square wavefront of tile outlines races out along the board's own grid. Each bot is hit when the wave reaches it: it glitches, its red optics go dark, and they flicker back on as it reboots.
- **Building and scrapping** share one purple language. A new piece's footprint lays in first, corner ticks then edges. Then a scanline climbs from the footprint's front corner at a steady speed, so a Turret prints quickly and an Auger takes its time, with sparks at both ends and the rows just printed glowing behind it. The piece prints dark. When it lands, a purple ring runs out with a puff of dust, the piece flashes purple and flickers on, and the outline steps away. Scrapping plays it backwards and quicker: the piece powers down in a purple wash, then a scanline eats it from the top, purple bits lifting off the line, and the outline steps out after it. Upgrades print in the same way. It runs on the real clock, so it plays out while paused.
- **Small things:** Lights stutter when power comes and goes, pulses run along the cables, idle turrets sweep, and each point of Lithium flies up into the counter.
- **The ground:** the cracked apron of an abandoned campus. The upper-left edge of each slab catches the light and the upper-right one falls into shadow. Moss and dry grass take back patches across tile edges, and a slow drift in value keeps the field uneven.
- **The unlit field:** it's shaded, never blurred, so the bot designs read clearly in the void. The shade steps along the board's own grid: a lighter ring of quarter-tile diamonds (8 × 4 px) just outside each light, and a deeper shade on half-tile diamonds (16 × 8 px) beyond it. Inside the light, and on every lit building, there's no shade.
- **Light on the ground:** the Base and servers lift the ground in three stepped bands (a near-neutral warm light, so the ground keeps its colour) and live pools cast two bands of green, on the same diamonds. Light lands on the ground, not on the buildings standing in it. There are no gradients on the board.
- **UI:** the chrome follows the board's rules. Every panel is a concrete instrument plate: one flat warm fill, the same 1px dark outline the sprites wear, a lit ledge along the top and a darker foot, and corners cut at 45°. Nothing is blurred. The name and the readouts share one console plate, split by an engraved groove. Pad pieces stand on small concrete slabs shaded like board tiles, and the chosen one is ringed in purple like the target tile. When a menu opens, the chrome steps away and the field breaks into a stepped mosaic behind the card. This is the only place the board is broken up.
- **Type:** Michroma (a free stand-in for New Science) is the voice of the station: the name, menu titles and eyebrows, readout labels and numbers, wave calls, the paused tag and the main button. Oxygen carries sentences, rules and pad labels. Both load from Google Fonts.

## Sound
Every cue is a short `.wav` in `sfx/`, mapped to events in the `SFX` table in `index.html`. Each cue has a level, a gap it can't restart inside, and for the busy ones a small random pitch drift, so many turrets firing don't all sound on one note.

| Cue | File | Plays on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| turret | `fire-turrets` | a turret firing |
| launch | `fire-missles` | a missile leaving its tube |
| botHit | `damage-bot` | any hit on a bot: rounds, missile splash, the EMP front |
| userHit | `damage-user` | a bot striking one of your buildings (louder on the Base) |
| pop | `explosion02` | a bot destroyed, a missile landing, a chunk blowing off a building |
| blast | `explosion01` | a Panzer going up, a building wrecked, the Base's chain of blasts (the last one deeper) |
| emp | `EMP` | firing the EMP |
| wave | `wave` | a wave incoming |
| rail | `fire-turrets` | a Railgun firing, played deep (rate 0.55) on its own channel |
| dart | `fire-missles` | each Ion Launcher dart leaving its cell, small and quick (gain 0.28, rate 1.3 and climbing through the swarm) |
| mech | `fire-turrets` | each round of a Warden's burst, quick and a touch high (rate 1.2) on its own channel; its missiles use `launch` at rate 1.15. It walks silently |
| blip | `ui-blip` | the only UI sound: picking a tool, pause, speed, ?, the field guide, Start / Again / Resume; scrapping and a refusal play it low, a wave held plays it high. Building a piece is silent |

On the web the cues play through Web Audio, which a browser only starts after a press, so the first tap or key unlocks it. Explosions go through a limiter, so many at once sound dense instead of clipping. Opened from disk, where browsers refuse `fetch`, the page falls back to `<audio>` elements. The iOS app plays the same cues natively (see the app's `Sound.swift`), as ambient audio: the ring switch silences it, and music from other apps keeps playing.
